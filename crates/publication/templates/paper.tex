\begin{abstract}
We describe an open-source synthetic data engine combining primitive-object scenes with furnished procedural interiors, calibrated multi-view cameras, optional human motion, and aligned geometric annotations. Indoor generation samples continuous architectural, furnishing, appearance, lighting and camera parameters under geometric constraints. A continuous camera-baseline control coordinates spacing, shared-surface overlap and path variation. The architectural evaluation audits \PubAuditRooms{} consecutive rooms and \PubViewCount{} aligned views across \PubRenderRooms{} rooms. \PubCameraStudySummary{} Exact configurations, capture-engine identities and reproducible artifacts accompany the measured cohorts. Photographic realism, unlimited-process stability and downstream pretraining gains are not established by these diagnostics.
\end{abstract}

\section{Purpose and scope}
Sparse-view reconstruction benefits from observations of shared surfaces: cameras that see unrelated objects offer little direct correspondence supervision. LRM-Zero demonstrates the potential of procedurally synthesized object data~\cite{xie2024}; MVSplat uses cross-view matching to estimate geometry~\cite{chen2024}. Our contribution is an inspectable data-generation system and its bounded evaluation, rather than a trained reconstruction model or a reconstruction accuracy result.

The engine supports Object, CornellCube, Room, SemanticRoom and Human scene modes alongside furnished interiors in \texttt{ProceduralIndoor}. The runtime uses Bevy 0.20, optional Burn 0.22 inference, and published AnnyBody/ARDY integrations. Office, conference, lounge, classroom, library and related activity priors influence a compositional program; they do not select complete meshes or a finite collection of room templates. Most architecture, furniture, plants and clutter require neither mesh repositories nor photographic texture collections. AnnyBody human meshes and optional motion/embedding models are explicit pretrained dependencies.

\section{Procedural scene program}
A scene is generated as $S=G(s,\theta)$, where $s$ seeds independent random streams and $\theta$ contains user-controlled densities, activity, quality, camera and motion policies. The serialized manifest records the sampled program, object transforms and seeds, people, photometry and camera paths. A separate generator version and capture-engine identity invalidate incompatible resumptions. Determinism means reproducing the same program and settings with compatible assets and engine versions; bit-identical rasterization across GPU vendors is not promised.

\subsection{Architecture, objects and appearance}
Room area and aspect, functional zones, aisle widths and activity mixtures are sampled continuously. The exterior footprint supports taper, chamfered corners and a concave cut-in. A shared architectural program controls polygon triangulation, wall apertures, sloping ceilings, arched portals, pillars, floor levels and furnished mezzanines with stairs and guards. Furniture support, camera clearance, fixture anchors and voxel export use the same surfaces. Windows vary aperture dimensions, sill/head heights, bay widths, frames, inset depth and shades across multiple exterior edges. An internal glazed connection retains a furnished neighboring room. This is a bounded architectural grammar: courtyard cut-ins remain on the rear facade, neighboring rooms and mezzanine decks remain rectangular, and arbitrary multi-storey connectivity, structural analysis and building-code compliance are outside scope.

Furniture is assembled from material-specific parts. Tables vary outline, taper, thickness and leg/pedestal construction; chairs vary seat shell, curvature, back proportions, recline, arms, base and headrest. Sectional seating, shelving, potted plants, outlets, switches, ceiling lights, whiteboards, displays and tabletop objects extend the semantic inventory. Placement uses support relationships, clearance checks, zone-dependent furnishing fields and bounded rejection. Chairs sample partial and deep insertion beneath tables, including occupied seats; the rendered leg, pedestal, brace and panel program also supplies collision geometry. Working poses mix free reaches with one- or two-palm tabletop support using fixed-length arm IK and skin-aware contact fitting. This is static surface support, not simulated grasping or animated contact dynamics. Surface clutter varies shared arrangement, handedness, spacing and local jitter. Object yaw and arrangement are sampled rather than restricted to orthogonal grids. Collision proxies approximate detailed meshes; geometric validity is checked separately from physical plausibility.

Surfaces use 35 role-specific base material slots with procedural albedo, normal and roughness maps, metric UV scales and mip chains. Recipes vary spatial frequency, directional grain, textile structure, mineral veining, relief, wear, color and roughness. Upholstery finish slots share three independently seeded structure programs per substrate; clothing reuses these maps with independent actor pigmentation and atlas scaling. Woven textiles sample over/under grammars, yarn diameter, slub, crimp, dyed warp/weft and fuzz. Leather varies grain spacing, stretch, pore relief, nap, pigmentation and polish. Coupled albedo, metric normal, cavity and roughness fields are baked into mipmapped textures. Textile directional GGX scattering approximates aggregate fibre reflection; it is not a measured cloth multiple-scattering BSDF. Mineral programs blend cement binder, exposed aggregate, pinholes and warped multiscale vein deposits with independent polish. Wall finishes vary roller stipple, sprayed and flattened plaster relief, trowel marks and gloss; glazed ceramics add restrained crackle and a protective lobe. Timber separates grain cuts from stain absorption, bleaching, pore fill and varnish; face and edge grain share their finish. Foliage atlases keep veins aligned to the blade fold and vary chlorophyll patches, pale variegation, wax and thin-surface transmission. Timber and foliage finish slots share two texture structures per role. Timber and stone floor atlases use 512-square maps to resolve separately cut boards and stone tiles; other substrates use 256-square maps. Screens, boards and printed pages have independently seeded content. Only referenced finish variants are built during runtime preparation. Glass recipes vary roughness, refractive index, tint, attenuation and thickness. Direct lights vary placement, intensity, color temperature, circuit activity and direction; exposure, daylight and sky radiance are independently recorded. Low-light samples are retained in evaluation.

Timber samples offset and tapered growth-ring cuts, uneven earlywood/latewood, elongated pores and sparse branch knots; floor planks have independent cuts. Unresolved normal variation contributes to filtered GGX roughness. Chrome retains a conductive base layer with continuously varied polished/brushed roughness and anisotropy; micron scratches are represented as scattering rather than quantized visible bumps. A static scene-derived HDR environment traces the rendered triangle assemblies and visibility-tested direct lights using the shared transport BVH. Linear radiance is stored in float16 cubemaps, with GGX specular and cosine diffuse convolution; source footprints suppress sparse-emitter filtering noise. Secondary bounce energy is approximate. One reflection origin does not provide per-object parallax, live moving-human reflections or a full specular path-tracing reference.

People retain AnnyBody geometry with continuous phenotype/pose parameters, fitted garments, surface hair and facial details. Static people remain the default. Opt-in ARDY motion combines seeded procedural text sequences with object-aware waypoint planning and scene-time playback. Requesting static scenes does not initialize motion inference. Motion models use the upstream loaders and caches; cached prompt text does not replace the motion seed. Clothing, hair, joint limits and collision handling are approximations, particularly during complex motion.

\subsection{Multi-view sampling and training defaults}
Camera centers and complete trajectories are constrained to the primary furnished room by default. The sampler varies focal length, position, target, roll and path shape, checks obstacle clearance and prefers visible content. Static capture uses one timestep; optional trajectories support repeated calibrated observations. A reference camera anchors a connected set of views. For each other camera and five trajectory times, we require
\[
 \min\{o_{0\rightarrow i}(t),o_{i\rightarrow0}(t)\}\geq\tau,
 \qquad b_{\min}\leq\|c_i(t)-c_0(t)\|\leq b_{\max},
\]
where $o$ estimates first-surface overlap using visibility rays and object/architecture proxies. Overlap constrains reference edges. An additional minimum separation applies to every camera pair at 33 synchronized path samples. Group spread is the horizontal camera-center covariance's minor/major standard-deviation ratio. Moving pairs must differ in normalized RMS displacement after removing their starting offsets. Full-path swept collision checks remain; sampled spread and overlap are not continuous-time or rendered-pixel guarantees.

The camera-baseline control $b\in[0,1]$ expands into explicit advanced settings: minimum pair separation $0.04+0.46b$ metres, minimum reference distance $0.06+2.30b$ metres, maximum reference distance $0.35+9.65b$ metres, overlap target $0.65-0.50b^{0.7}$, group spread $0.20+0.10b$, independent path variation $b$, and visible-region target variation $b$. The default is $b=0.5$. Wide settings permit distant viewpoints and lower overlap; narrow settings favor close views of shared content. Collision and visibility rejection affect the realized distribution. Arbitrary room, camera-count and constraint combinations need not be feasible.

Peers can aim at different first-surface proxy hits visible to the reference camera. Target interpolation and proposal frequency increase with baseline; frequency also increases with camera count. Feasible candidates are ranked for nearest-peer distance, optical-axis separation and additional quantized primary-room surface cells. Preferred distance scales with room diagonal divided by the square root of camera count; it is not a hard packing constraint. Selection considers at most 2, 3 or 4 feasible candidates for groups of 2--4, 5--8 or 9+ views, within a 12-proposal window after the first feasible candidate and the existing total search budget. Zero target variation retains shared-target, first-fit rigs. Rare completion for 2--11 views can use one bounded overcomplete search, retaining the reference and rechecking every subset against the same geometric constraints. All selected views still satisfy the original collision, content, distance and overlap gates. Proxy surface-cell novelty does not measure exact mesh area or reconstruction gains. Related temporal routes and occasional shared-target recovery remain supported; varied gaze does not imply independent route topology.

The inspector exposes this slider above the advanced controls. Any advanced edit switches to explicit custom values. Changes take effect on regeneration, so dragging a control does not rebuild the room. JSON input accepts \texttt{\{"baseline":0.75\}} within the \texttt{indoor\_camera} configuration. Serialization records the expanded values, avoiding hidden preset precedence. Camera travel length and long-route frequency are separate from spacing between cameras. Joint collision and overlap rejection can favor shorter accepted routes at wide spacing; the minimum travel bound remains explicit. The finite search budget reports impossible settings without silently weakening them.

\section{Capture and annotation contract}
Capture begins only after scene preparation, assets, shader pipelines, lighting and requested human trajectories are ready. A bounded asynchronous readback protocol associates outputs with the requested scene, camera, time and mode; it does not infer readiness from an arbitrary number of frames. Sequential native indoor streams automatically prepare future rooms on a shared bounded CPU pool and can upload one future room's immutable assets during current readback. Future entities, lighting dispatches and motion inference remain gated by scene promotion. This scheduling retains full native quality rather than reducing shadows or geometry. Geometric channels share an RGBA32Float ground-truth raster attachment. RGB uses Bevy's PBR pipeline, shadow maps, tone mapping and optional indirect-lighting approximation. Float32 storage does not imply that all RGB or lighting computations have float32 intermediate precision: HDR color and irradiance volumes include float16 stages.

Headless generation supports chunked safetensors or per-sample files, compression, resumable identity checks, metrics and bounded worker replacement. RGB and numeric channels have separate encoding contracts. Native generation performs numeric temporal export and O-Voxel export. The WebGPU viewer supports indoor construction, inspection and optional motion inference, using the same manifest and geometry modules; Portable quality reduces unsupported or costly rendering features. Browser velocity visualization is not a qualification of native dataset flow export. The current Wasm path omits native baked diffuse GI and uses its platform lighting fallback.

\subsection{Co-visibility representation and surface agreement}
Co-visibility shares the float32 surface raster used by the other geometric annotations. For source camera $s$, pixel $p$ and time $t$, the exported unsigned 16-bit integer is
\[
 M_s(p,t)=\sum_{\substack{j=0\\j\ne s}}^{N-1}2^j V_{s\rightarrow j}(p,t),
 \qquad 1\leq N\leq16,
\]
where $V_{s\rightarrow j}\in\{0,1\}$ records visibility of the source surface in peer camera $j$. The source camera's own bit is always zero; the editor has no membership bit. The ordered camera legend remains identical across views and timesteps in a sample. A peer's binary supervision is recovered by testing $(M_s\mathbin{\&}2^j)\ne0$; the number of observing peers is $\operatorname{popcount}(M_s)$. Thus the integer preserves camera identities, unlike a shared-pixel count alone.

The GPU pass projects a valid source surface into each peer, checks near/far clipping and image bounds, and reads the nearest target pixel. For source point $x$, target point $y$, corresponding world-space unit normals $n_s,n_j$ and target axial depth $d$, the surface-agreement test is
\[
 \max\!\left(\left|(y-x)\cdot n_s\right|,
                    \left|(y-x)\cdot n_j\right|\right)
 \leq \max(0.001\,\mathrm{m},10^{-4}d).
\]
This local-plane test avoids comparing depths along different pixel-center rays on sloped surfaces. It remains resolution- and tolerance-dependent: silhouettes, thin geometry, curved surfaces and near-contact surfaces can be ambiguous. Pixel-level membership need not be symmetric after raster reprojection. The chosen solid or through-glass annotation policy applies to the source and every peer. Reflected and refracted RGB content does not imply co-visible geometry.

The additive RGB8 preview sums the contributions of set camera bits without tone mapping. For four cameras, the shared legend is $C_0=(170,0,0)$, $C_1=(0,255,0)$, $C_2=(0,0,255)$ and $C_3=(85,0,0)$. For example, $M=10=\texttt{0b1010}$ means peers 1 and 3, with color $(85,255,0)$. These colors encode camera membership, not semantic classes or overlap magnitude. The palette adapts deterministically to camera count and remains uniquely decodable for all supported combinations in the original lossless PNG.

A separate uint8 source-valid mask $A_s$ distinguishes an unshared surface ($A_s=1,M_s=0$) from background ($A_s=0,M_s=0$); both appear black in the additive preview. Folder exports retain membership and validity as $[H,W,1]$ arrays in NPZ. Chunked exports use $[B,T,N,H,W,1]$. Training should consume these numeric arrays and the camera metadata, rather than rasterizing the PDF or decoding a resized or lossy preview. Shared percentages divide by valid source-pixel observations, including unshared surfaces, not unique 3D points.

\section{Limitations}
Procedural correctness, broad parameter variation and efficient capture do not establish photographic realism. Hair is a surface groom, skin lacks a full subsurface model, some furnishings remain sparse, object motion uses approximate collision proxies, and diffuse GI uses probes and simplified transport. Glass and lighting are approximations rather than full path tracing. Physical transport accuracy and visual plausibility require independent path-traced reference studies; the camera-baseline sweep does not measure either.

The current study is a generator audit and a small capture cohort, not a ten-million-scene run. It does not establish long-run resident-memory bounds, balanced coverage of all rare architectural combinations, collision-free arbitrary motion, suitability for every reconstruction loss, or transfer to real photographs. Shared-view defaults deliberately correlate cameras within a room; train/validation splits should therefore be made by room seed, not by image. A downstream assessment should freeze a training budget and real evaluation set, compare matched data quantities and camera policies, and measure reconstruction/calibration quality alongside redundancy. The manifests and metrics make that experiment reproducible without claiming its outcome in advance.
