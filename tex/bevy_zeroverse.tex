\begin{abstract}
We describe an open-source synthetic data engine combining primitive-object scenes with furnished procedural interiors, calibrated multi-view cameras, optional human motion, and aligned geometric annotations. Indoor generation samples continuous architectural, furnishing, appearance, lighting and camera parameters under geometric constraints. A continuous camera-baseline control coordinates spacing, shared-surface overlap and path variation. The architectural evaluation audits 512 consecutive rooms and 256 aligned views across 32 rooms. A separately identified camera-baseline reference study covers \CameraAuditRooms{} layouts and \CameraRenderRooms{} rendered rooms, with co-visibility measurements at five baseline values. Current capabilities and measured cohorts are distinguished, with exact configurations and reproducible artifacts. Photographic realism, unlimited-process stability and downstream pretraining gains are not established by these diagnostics.
\end{abstract}

\section{Purpose and scope}
Sparse-view reconstruction benefits from observations of shared surfaces: cameras that see unrelated objects offer little direct correspondence supervision. LRM-Zero demonstrates the potential of procedurally synthesized object data~\cite{xie2024}; MVSplat uses cross-view matching to estimate geometry~\cite{chen2024}. Our contribution is an inspectable data-generation system and its bounded evaluation, rather than a trained reconstruction model or a reconstruction accuracy result.

The engine retains the existing Object, CornellCube, Room, SemanticRoom and Human scene modes, and exposes furnished interiors separately as \texttt{ProceduralIndoor}. The runtime uses Bevy 0.19.1, optional Burn 0.21 inference, and published AnnyBody/ARDY integrations. Office, conference, lounge, classroom, library and related activity priors influence a compositional program; they do not select complete meshes or a finite collection of room templates. Most architecture, furniture, plants and clutter require neither mesh repositories nor photographic texture collections. AnnyBody human meshes and optional motion/embedding models are explicit pretrained dependencies.

\section{Procedural scene program}
A scene is generated as $S=G(s,\theta)$, where $s$ seeds independent random streams and $\theta$ contains user-controlled densities, activity, quality, camera and motion policies. The serialized manifest records the sampled program, object transforms and seeds, people, photometry and camera paths. A separate generator version and capture-engine identity invalidate incompatible resumptions. Determinism means reproducing the same program and settings with compatible assets and engine versions; bit-identical rasterization across GPU vendors is not promised.

\subsection{Architecture, objects and appearance}
Room area and aspect, functional zones, aisle widths and activity mixtures are sampled continuously. The exterior footprint supports taper, chamfered corners and a concave cut-in. A shared architectural program controls polygon triangulation, wall apertures, sloping ceilings, arched portals, pillars, floor levels and furnished mezzanines with stairs and guards. Furniture support, camera clearance, fixture anchors and voxel export use the same surfaces. Windows vary aperture dimensions, sill/head heights, bay widths, frames, inset depth and shades across multiple exterior edges. An internal glazed connection retains a furnished neighboring room. This is a bounded architectural grammar: courtyard cut-ins remain on the rear facade, neighboring rooms and mezzanine decks remain rectangular, and arbitrary multi-storey connectivity, structural analysis and building-code compliance are outside scope.

Furniture is assembled from material-specific parts. Tables vary outline, taper, thickness and leg/pedestal construction; chairs vary seat shell, curvature, back proportions, recline, arms, base and headrest. Sectional seating, shelving, potted plants, outlets, switches, ceiling lights, whiteboards, displays and tabletop objects extend the semantic inventory. Placement uses support relationships, clearance checks, zone-dependent furnishing fields and bounded rejection. Object yaw and arrangement are sampled rather than restricted to orthogonal grids. Collision proxies approximate detailed meshes; geometric validity is checked separately from physical plausibility.

Surfaces use 35 role-specific base material slots with procedural albedo, normal and roughness maps, metric UV scales and mip chains. Recipes vary spatial frequency, directional grain, textile structure, mineral veining, relief, wear, color and roughness. Object finish variants preserve their underlying maps while varying appearance; screens, boards and printed pages have independently seeded content. Only referenced finish variants are built during runtime preparation. Glass recipes vary roughness, refractive index, tint, attenuation and thickness. Direct lights vary placement, intensity, color temperature, circuit activity and direction; exposure, daylight and sky radiance are independently recorded. Low-light samples are retained in evaluation.

People retain AnnyBody geometry with continuous phenotype/pose parameters, fitted garments, surface hair and facial details. Static people remain the default. Opt-in ARDY motion combines seeded procedural text sequences with object-aware waypoint planning and scene-time playback. Requesting static scenes does not initialize motion inference. Motion models use the upstream loaders and caches; cached prompt text does not replace the motion seed. Clothing, hair, joint limits and collision handling are approximations, particularly during complex motion.

\subsection{Multi-view sampling and training defaults}
Camera centers and complete trajectories are constrained to the primary furnished room by default. The sampler varies focal length, position, target, roll and path shape, checks obstacle clearance and prefers visible content. Static capture uses one timestep; optional trajectories support repeated calibrated observations. A reference camera anchors a connected set of views. For each other camera and five trajectory times, we require
\[
 \min\{o_{0\rightarrow i}(t),o_{i\rightarrow0}(t)\}\geq\tau,
 \qquad b_{\min}\leq\|c_i(t)-c_0(t)\|\leq b_{\max},
\]
where $o$ estimates first-surface overlap using visibility rays and object/architecture proxies. Overlap constrains reference edges. An additional minimum separation applies to every camera pair at 33 synchronized path samples. Group spread is the horizontal camera-center covariance's minor/major standard-deviation ratio. Moving pairs must differ in normalized RMS displacement after removing their starting offsets. Full-path swept collision checks remain; sampled spread and overlap are not continuous-time or rendered-pixel guarantees.

The camera-baseline control $b\in[0,1]$ expands into explicit advanced settings: minimum pair separation $0.04+0.46b$ metres, minimum reference distance $0.06+2.30b$ metres, maximum reference distance $0.35+9.65b$ metres, overlap target $0.65-0.55b^{0.6}$, group spread $0.20+0.10b$, and independent path variation $b$. The default is $b=0.5$. Wide settings permit distant viewpoints and lower overlap; narrow settings favor close views of shared content. Collision and visibility rejection affect the realized distribution. Arbitrary room, camera-count and constraint combinations need not be feasible.

The default grouped sampler remains biased toward a shared fixation region: peer targets inherit the reference target with only $\pm0.35$ m horizontal and $\pm0.15$ m vertical jitter. Their endpoint targets inherit the same offset. Baseline changes positional spacing and path variation but does not broaden this target jitter. Positional diversity and overlap checks therefore do not establish independent gaze or convergence diversity.

The inspector exposes this slider above the advanced controls. Any advanced edit switches to explicit custom values. Changes take effect on regeneration, so dragging a control does not rebuild the room. JSON input accepts \texttt{\{"baseline":0.75\}} within the \texttt{indoor\_camera} configuration. Serialization records the expanded values, avoiding hidden preset precedence. Camera travel length and long-route frequency are separate from spacing between cameras. Joint collision and overlap rejection can favor shorter accepted routes at wide spacing; the minimum travel bound remains explicit. The finite search budget reports impossible settings without silently weakening them.

\section{Capture and annotation contract}
Capture begins only after scene preparation, assets, shader pipelines, lighting and requested human trajectories are ready. A bounded asynchronous readback protocol associates outputs with the requested scene, camera, time and mode; it does not infer readiness from an arbitrary number of frames. Geometric channels share an RGBA32Float ground-truth raster attachment. RGB uses Bevy's PBR pipeline, shadow maps, tone mapping and optional indirect-lighting approximation. Float32 storage does not imply that all RGB or lighting computations have float32 intermediate precision: HDR color and irradiance volumes include float16 stages.

\section{Current architectural evaluation}
\subsection{Architectural audit}
The current architectural program was audited on 512 consecutive seeds (0--511), with four cameras, furnishing density 0.65 and static human density 0.25. Every room passed layout, support, clearance and camera checks. Oblique walls occurred in 458 rooms, chamfered corners in 307, exterior cut-ins in 221, sloped ceilings in 354, raised floors in 90, sunken floors in 103, pillars in 425, arches in 194 and furnished mezzanines in 46. All 512 structural signatures were distinct after quantizing dimensions to 1 cm; this bounded uniqueness does not establish ten-million-sample utility.

The first 32 rooms were rendered without filtering: four cameras at two trajectory endpoints produced 256 views in RGB, depth, normal, semantic and position modes at $640\times400$. All views passed annotation alignment. Maximum per-view depth/position p99 discrepancy was $2.87\,\mu$m, reprojection p99 was 0.00264 pixels, and decoded normal-length error was $2.39\times10^{-7}$. These are annotation consistency checks, not real-world accuracy measurements. The \href{https://github.com/mosure/bevy_zeroverse/blob/main/docs/architecture_v22.md}{architectural report} retains generator-22 identities, complete contact sheets and machine-readable distributions.

\subsection{Aligned images of the current geometry}
The main project-page explorer contains all 32 consecutively captured rooms, four cameras and normalized times $t=0,1$: 256 camera/time identities and 1,280 displayed channel images. RGB, depth, normals, semantics and positions switch atomically with the corresponding calibration and plan. These are existing native captures of the current generator; rebuilding the page does not perform a new render. Figure~\ref{fig:architecture-annotations} retains pixel alignment and illustrates several different floor levels.

The render configuration is Auto quality, shadows, SSAO and approximate diffuse GI at 1024 primary rays per probe, using native Vulkan on an RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell. RGB display images preserve renderer tone mapping followed by sRGB encoding; no per-image exposure correction, crop or appearance filtering is applied. Depth previews are 8-bit grayscale axial depth divided by 15 m and clamped. Normal previews store $(n+1)/2$; position previews affine-normalize world coordinates by the annotation AABB and clamp for display. Semantic colors are preserved exactly. Non-RGB PNGs are converted losslessly to WebP, verified by decoded-pixel equality; these 8-bit previews must not be treated as float32 training labels. Original capture checks used the direct float32 ground-truth attachment. Calibration and program downloads retain exact input identities and original PNG hashes.

Every captured view contains at least four semantic classes. All 18 rooms containing people in the camera's primary functional zone show person pixels in at least one captured view. Across the wider main envelope, 22 rooms contain people; three never show person pixels (seeds 17, 24 and 31). These are class-level observations across both endpoints, not individual-person visibility guarantees. Scene construction through capture/export takes 1.30--4.41 seconds per room (median 2.05), excluding application startup; each room produces eight views in five modes. This is a bounded run, not a sustained-throughput or memory-stability qualification.

\section{Limitations and remaining evidence}
Procedural correctness, broad parameter variation and efficient capture do not establish photographic realism. Hair is a surface groom, skin lacks a full subsurface model, some furnishings remain sparse, object motion uses approximate collision proxies, and diffuse GI uses probes and simplified transport. Glass and lighting are approximations rather than full path tracing. Physical transport accuracy and visual plausibility require independent path-traced reference studies; the camera-baseline sweep does not measure either.

The current study is a generator audit and a small capture cohort, not a ten-million-scene run. It does not establish long-run resident-memory bounds, balanced coverage of all rare architectural combinations, collision-free arbitrary motion, suitability for every reconstruction loss, or transfer to real photographs. Shared-view defaults deliberately correlate cameras within a room; train/validation splits should therefore be made by room seed, not by image. A downstream assessment should freeze a training budget and real evaluation set, compare matched data quantities and camera policies, and measure reconstruction/calibration quality alongside redundancy. The manifests and metrics make that experiment reproducible without claiming its outcome in advance.

\subsection{Co-visibility renderings and camera-specific supervision}
Figures~\ref{fig:co-visibility-views} and~\ref{fig:co-visibility-bits} show the production GPU annotation for the matched reference gallery. These are actual exported surface-membership masks at the same camera poses, intrinsics and time as the RGB images. They are distinct from the looser depth-based population diagnostic in the baseline table. The figures use the existing selected captures; they add no rooms to the population denominator.

Co-visibility shares the float32 surface raster used by the other geometric annotations. For source camera $s$, pixel $p$ and time $t$, the exported unsigned 16-bit integer is
\[
 M_s(p,t)=\sum_{\substack{j=0\\j\ne s}}^{N-1}2^j V_{s\rightarrow j}(p,t),
 \qquad 1\leq N\leq16,
\]
where $V_{s\rightarrow j}\in\{0,1\}$ records visibility of the source surface in peer camera $j$. The source camera's own bit is always zero; the editor has no membership bit. The ordered camera legend remains identical across views and timesteps in a sample. A peer's binary supervision is recovered by testing $(M_s\mathbin{\&}2^j)\ne0$; the number of observing peers is $\operatorname{popcount}(M_s)$. Thus the integer preserves camera identities, unlike a shared-pixel count alone.

The GPU pass projects a valid source surface into each peer, checks near/far clipping and image bounds, and reads the nearest target pixel. For source point $x$, target point $y$, corresponding world-space unit normals $n_s,n_j$ and target axial depth $d$, the surface-agreement test is
\[
 \max\!\left(\left|(y-x)\cdot n_s\right|,
                    \left|(y-x)\cdot n_j\right|\right)
 \leq \max(0.001\,\mathrm{m},10^{-4}d).
\]
This local-plane test avoids comparing depths along different pixel-center rays on sloped surfaces. It remains resolution- and tolerance-dependent: silhouettes, thin geometry, curved surfaces and near-contact surfaces can be ambiguous. Pixel-level membership need not be symmetric after raster reprojection. Glass is the first opaque geometric surface, so reflected, refracted and transmitted RGB content does not imply co-visible geometry.

The additive RGB8 preview sums the contributions of set camera bits without tone mapping. For four cameras, the shared legend is $C_0=(170,0,0)$, $C_1=(0,255,0)$, $C_2=(0,0,255)$ and $C_3=(85,0,0)$. For example, $M=10=\texttt{0b1010}$ means peers 1 and 3, with color $(85,255,0)$. These colors encode camera membership, not semantic classes or overlap magnitude. The palette adapts deterministically to camera count and remains uniquely decodable for all supported combinations in the original lossless PNG.

A separate uint8 source-valid mask $A_s$ distinguishes an unshared surface ($A_s=1,M_s=0$) from background ($A_s=0,M_s=0$); both appear black in the additive preview. Folder exports retain membership and validity as $[H,W,1]$ arrays in NPZ. Chunked exports use $[B,T,N,H,W,1]$. Training should consume these numeric arrays and the camera metadata, rather than rasterizing the PDF or decoding a resized or lossy preview. Shared percentages below divide by valid source-pixel observations, including unshared surfaces, not unique 3D points.
