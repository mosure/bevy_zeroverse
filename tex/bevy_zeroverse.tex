\begin{abstract}
We describe an open-source synthetic data engine combining primitive-object scenes with furnished procedural interiors, calibrated multi-view cameras, optional human motion, and aligned geometric annotations. Indoor generation samples continuous architectural, furnishing, appearance, lighting and camera parameters under geometric constraints. A continuous camera-baseline control coordinates spacing, shared-surface overlap and path variation. The architectural evaluation audits \PubAuditRooms{} consecutive rooms and \PubViewCount{} aligned views across \PubRenderRooms{} rooms. \PubCameraStudySummary{} Exact configurations, capture-engine identities and reproducible artifacts accompany the measured cohorts. Photographic realism, unlimited-process stability and downstream pretraining gains are not established by these diagnostics.
\end{abstract}

\section{Purpose and scope}
Sparse-view reconstruction benefits from observations of shared surfaces: cameras that see unrelated objects offer little direct correspondence supervision. LRM-Zero demonstrates the potential of procedurally synthesized object data~\cite{xie2024}; MVSplat uses cross-view matching to estimate geometry~\cite{chen2024}. Our contribution is an inspectable data-generation system and its bounded evaluation, rather than a trained reconstruction model or a reconstruction accuracy result.

The engine supports Object, CornellCube, Room, SemanticRoom and Human scene modes alongside furnished interiors in \texttt{ProceduralIndoor}. The runtime uses Bevy 0.20, optional Burn 0.22 inference, and published AnnyBody/ARDY integrations. Office, conference, lounge, classroom, library and related activity priors influence a compositional program; they do not select complete meshes or a finite collection of room templates. Most architecture, furniture, plants and clutter require neither mesh repositories nor photographic texture collections. AnnyBody human meshes and optional motion/embedding models are explicit pretrained dependencies.

\section{Procedural scene program}
A scene is generated as $S=G(s,\theta)$, where $s$ seeds independent random streams and $\theta$ contains user-controlled densities, activity, quality, camera and motion policies. The serialized manifest records the sampled program, object transforms and seeds, people, photometry and camera paths. A separate generator version and capture-engine identity invalidate incompatible resumptions. Determinism means reproducing the same program and settings with compatible assets and engine versions; bit-identical rasterization across GPU vendors is not promised.

\subsection{Architecture, objects and appearance}
Room area and aspect, functional zones, aisle widths and activity mixtures are sampled continuously. The exterior footprint supports taper, chamfered corners, a concave cut-in and up to two offset returns on eligible side edges. Accepted returns preserve the complete shared wall to the neighboring room and at least 78\% of the original footprint area. A shared architectural program controls polygon triangulation, wall apertures, sloping ceilings, closed soffits, arched portals, pillars, floor levels and furnished mezzanines with stairs and guards. Furniture support, camera clearance, fixture anchors and voxel export use the same surfaces, including local headroom beneath soffits. Windows vary aperture dimensions, sill/head heights, bay widths, frames, inset depth and shades across multiple exterior edges. An internal glazed connection retains a furnished neighboring room. This is a bounded architectural grammar: footprints are connected simple polygons without interior holes, neighboring rooms and mezzanine decks remain rectangular, and arbitrary multi-storey connectivity, structural analysis and building-code compliance are outside scope.

Furniture is assembled from material-specific parts. Tables vary outline, taper, thickness and leg/pedestal construction; chairs vary seat shell, curvature, back proportions, recline, arms, base and headrest. Sectional seating, shelving, potted plants, outlets, switches, ceiling lights, whiteboards, displays and tabletop objects extend the semantic inventory. Placement uses support relationships, clearance checks, zone-dependent furnishing fields and bounded rejection. Chairs sample partial and deep insertion beneath tables, including occupied seats; the rendered leg, pedestal, brace and panel program also supplies collision geometry. Working poses mix free reaches with one- or two-palm tabletop support using fixed-length arm IK and skin-aware contact fitting. This is static surface support, not simulated grasping or animated contact dynamics. Surface clutter varies shared arrangement, handedness, spacing and local jitter. Object yaw and arrangement are sampled rather than restricted to orthogonal grids. Collision proxies approximate detailed meshes; geometric validity is checked separately from physical plausibility.

Surfaces use 36 role-specific base material slots with procedural albedo, normal and roughness maps, metric UV scales and mip chains. Recipes vary spatial frequency, directional grain, textile structure, mineral veining, relief, wear, color and roughness. Upholstery finish slots share three independently seeded structure programs per substrate; clothing reuses these maps with independent actor pigmentation and atlas scaling. Woven textiles sample over/under grammars, yarn diameter, slub, crimp, dyed warp/weft and fuzz. Leather varies grain spacing, stretch, pore relief, nap, pigmentation and polish. Coupled albedo, metric normal, cavity and roughness fields are baked into mipmapped textures. Textile directional GGX scattering approximates aggregate fibre reflection; it is not a measured cloth multiple-scattering BSDF. Mineral programs blend cement binder, exposed aggregate, pinholes and warped multiscale vein deposits with independent polish. Wall finishes vary roller stipple, sprayed and flattened plaster relief, trowel marks and gloss; glazed ceramics add restrained crackle and a protective lobe. Timber separates grain cuts from stain absorption, bleaching, pore fill and varnish; face and edge grain share their finish. Foliage atlases keep veins aligned to the blade fold and vary chlorophyll patches, pale variegation, wax and thin-surface transmission. Timber and foliage finish slots share two texture structures per role; metallic, chrome and plastic finishes use three continuously varied structure programs. Paint, neutral knit and the other supported substrates bake their maps on the GPU once per scene, retaining CPU recipe oracles and coupled normal/roughness mipmaps. Timber and stone floor atlases use 512-square maps to resolve separately cut boards and stone tiles; other substrates use 256-square maps. Screens, boards and printed pages have independently seeded content. Only referenced finish variants are built during runtime preparation. Glass recipes vary roughness, refractive index, tint, attenuation and thickness. Direct lights vary placement, intensity, color temperature, circuit activity and direction; exposure, daylight and sky radiance are independently recorded. Low-light samples are retained in evaluation.

Timber samples offset and tapered growth-ring cuts, uneven earlywood/latewood, elongated pores and sparse branch knots; floor planks have independent cuts. Unresolved normal variation contributes to filtered GGX roughness. Chrome retains a conductive base layer with continuously varied polished/brushed roughness and anisotropy; micron scratches are represented as scattering rather than quantized visible bumps. A static scene-derived HDR environment traces the rendered triangle assemblies and visibility-tested direct lights using the shared transport BVH. Linear radiance is stored in float16 cubemaps, with GGX specular and cosine diffuse convolution; source footprints suppress sparse-emitter filtering noise. Secondary bounce energy is approximate. Native rendering uses at most three box-projected zone probes and a global fallback; WebGPU uses the global environment because localized probes require texture-array capabilities. These bounded probes improve spatial reflection placement but do not provide live moving-human reflections or full specular path tracing. Their cosine/GGX convolutions execute once on the GPU with a CPU oracle; the static radiance tracing remains on the CPU.

People retain AnnyBody geometry with continuous phenotype/pose parameters, fitted garments, surface hair and facial details. Static people remain the default. Opt-in ARDY motion combines seeded procedural text sequences with object-aware waypoint planning and scene-time playback. Requesting static scenes does not initialize motion inference. Motion models use the upstream loaders and caches; cached prompt text does not replace the motion seed. Clothing, hair, joint limits and collision handling are approximations, particularly during complex motion.

\subsection{Multi-view sampling and training defaults}
Camera centers and complete trajectories are constrained to the primary furnished room by default. The sampler varies focal length, position, target, roll and path shape, checks obstacle clearance and prefers visible content. Static capture uses one timestep; optional trajectories support repeated calibrated observations. The configured overlap graph and mixed-rig policy account for calibrated sensor footprints. A reference camera supplies shared-content proposals; peer-aware candidate ranking and bounded gaze refinement also consider overlap between nonreference cameras. Placement overlap is a finite first-surface proxy, not rendered-pixel overlap or a continuous-time guarantee. Full-path swept collision checks remain. Group spread measures horizontal camera-center covariance anisotropy, while moving-pair residuals measure variation after removing initial offsets. The audit retains each request's expanded controls, mixed-rig contract, coherence targets and actual directed overlap diagnostics.

The camera-baseline control $b\in[0,1]$ expands into explicit advanced settings: minimum pair separation $0.04+0.46b$ metres, minimum reference distance $0.06+2.30b$ metres, maximum reference distance $0.35+9.65b$ metres, overlap target $0.65-0.50b^{0.7}$, group spread $0.20+0.10b$, independent path variation $b$, and visible-region target variation $b$. The default is $b=0.5$. Wide settings permit distant viewpoints and lower overlap; narrow settings favor close views of shared content. Collision and visibility rejection affect the realized distribution. Arbitrary room, camera-count and constraint combinations need not be feasible.

Peers can aim at different first-surface proxy hits visible to the reference camera. Feasible candidates are ranked for peer spacing, optical-axis separation and additional primary-room surface cells. The baseline preset also supports bounded framing refinement toward peer-overlap and containment targets, with caps for different lens footprints. These are search preferences, not a claim that every directed pair must meet the nominal overlap target. Bounded fallback preserves geometrically valid rooms when preferred rigs cannot be completed. The rendered study measures the resulting directed pair distribution separately, including weak or disjoint pairs. No image-quality filtering removes hard rooms from the prespecified population.

The inspector exposes this slider above the advanced controls. Any advanced edit switches to explicit custom values. Changes take effect on regeneration, so dragging a control does not rebuild the room. JSON input accepts \texttt{\{"baseline":0.75\}} within the \texttt{indoor\_camera} configuration. Serialization records the expanded values, avoiding hidden preset precedence. Camera travel length and long-route frequency are separate from spacing between cameras. Joint collision and overlap rejection can favor shorter accepted routes at wide spacing; the minimum travel bound remains explicit. The finite search budget reports impossible settings without silently weakening them.

\section{Capture and annotation contract}
Capture begins only after scene preparation, assets, shader pipelines, lighting and requested human trajectories are ready. A bounded asynchronous readback protocol associates outputs with the requested scene, camera, time and mode; it does not infer readiness from an arbitrary number of frames. Sequential native indoor streams automatically prepare future rooms on a shared bounded CPU pool and can upload one future room's immutable assets during current readback, preserving the RGB settling boundary. Speculative material and indirect-lighting compute waits until every current capture copy is submitted. Future entities remain excluded from the current capture. Between ordinary live requests, one declared successor can complete preparation with capture cameras disabled; this consumes the existing lookahead budget. This scheduling retains full native quality rather than reducing shadows or geometry. Geometric channels share an RGBA32Float ground-truth raster attachment. RGB uses Bevy's PBR pipeline, shadow maps, tone mapping and optional indirect-lighting approximation. Float32 storage does not imply that all RGB or lighting computations have float32 intermediate precision: HDR color and irradiance volumes include float16 stages.

Capture-camera supersampling defaults to an automatic policy: images below
$1280\times720$ pixels use a $2\times$ linear raster scale, while larger images
use native resolution. Explicit off, $2\times$, $3\times$ and $4\times$ overrides
are available. A GPU box resolve averages RGB in linear light after tone mapping
into the requested output dimensions, without a CPU filtering round trip.
Numeric depth, normals, position, semantics, flow and co-visibility retain the
exact final pixel-center rays. They are not averaged across surfaces: RGB can
have mixed edge coverage while its geometric target describes the central ray.
Calibration and flow units describe the final grid; versioned sampling metadata
records the resolved factor. Filtered annotation previews are display products,
not replacements for these geometric targets. Supersampling increases raster
work and GPU attachment memory without increasing final capture readback bytes.

\subsection{Co-visibility representation and surface agreement}
Co-visibility shares the float32 surface raster used by the other geometric annotations. For source camera $s$, pixel $p$ and time $t$, the exported unsigned 16-bit integer is
\[
 M_s(p,t)=\sum_{\substack{j=0\\j\ne s}}^{N-1}2^j V_{s\rightarrow j}(p,t),
 \qquad 1\leq N\leq16,
\]
where $V_{s\rightarrow j}\in\{0,1\}$ records visibility of the source surface in peer camera $j$. The source camera's own bit is always zero; the editor has no membership bit. The ordered camera legend remains identical across views and timesteps in a sample. A peer's binary supervision is recovered by testing $(M_s\mathbin{\&}2^j)\ne0$; the number of observing peers is $\operatorname{popcount}(M_s)$. Thus the integer preserves camera identities, unlike a shared-pixel count alone.

The GPU pass projects a valid source surface into each peer, checks near/far clipping and image bounds, and reads the nearest target pixel. For source point $x$, target point $y$, corresponding world-space unit normals $n_s,n_j$ and target axial depth $d$, the surface-agreement test is
\[
 \max\!\left(\left|(y-x)\cdot n_s\right|,
                    \left|(y-x)\cdot n_j\right|\right)
 \leq \max(0.001\,\mathrm{m},10^{-4}d).
\]
This local-plane test avoids comparing depths along different pixel-center rays on sloped surfaces. It remains resolution- and tolerance-dependent: silhouettes, thin geometry, curved surfaces and near-contact surfaces can be ambiguous. Pixel-level membership need not be symmetric after raster reprojection. The chosen solid or through-glass annotation policy applies to the source and every peer. Reflected and refracted RGB content does not imply co-visible geometry.

The additive RGB8 preview sums the contributions of set camera bits without tone mapping. For four cameras, the shared legend is $C_0=(170,0,0)$, $C_1=(0,255,0)$, $C_2=(0,0,255)$ and $C_3=(85,0,0)$. For example, $M=10=\texttt{0b1010}$ means peers 1 and 3, with color $(85,255,0)$. These colors encode camera membership, not semantic classes or overlap magnitude. The palette adapts deterministically to camera count and remains uniquely decodable for all supported combinations in the original lossless PNG.

A separate uint8 source-valid mask $A_s$ distinguishes an unshared surface ($A_s=1,M_s=0$) from background ($A_s=0,M_s=0$); both appear black in the additive preview. Folder exports retain membership and validity as $[H,W,1]$ arrays in NPZ. Chunked exports use $[B,T,N,H,W,1]$. Training should consume these numeric arrays and the camera metadata, rather than rasterizing the PDF or decoding a resized or lossy preview. Shared percentages divide by valid source-pixel observations, including unshared surfaces, not unique 3D points.

\section{Procedural capacity and sampling scale}
\label{sec:capacity}
The number of seed values, the number of named object categories, and the number
of useful training examples are different quantities. We define a
configuration-conditioned capacity framework and exact analytical reference
cases. We do not estimate the generator's total perceptual support or a
downstream learning exponent from the finite cohorts in this paper.

\subsection{Population, unit and distinguishability}
Fix resolved settings $C$, engine and asset identity $E$, and seed law $\pi$.
Generation $G_E(C,s)$ includes its bounded search and recovery policy. Report
success yield $a(C)$ separately from the distribution of accepted outputs;
failed rooms are not useful diversity classes. An observation descriptor is
$X=\Phi(\mathcal R_E(G_E(C,s)))$, conditional on success, where $\mathcal R$
includes the declared views, times, resolution, exposure and annotation policy.
A frozen partition $Q_\epsilon$ assigns observations to distinguishable classes
with probabilities $p_j=\Pr[Q_\epsilon(X)=j\mid\mathrm{success}]$.
The representation and resolution are part of the result, not hidden defaults.

A structural room family, dressed scene, multi-view capture and temporal clip
are different sample units. Camera, material and time variants can add useful
observations without adding independent structural families. Confidence intervals
and train/validation splits must retain this grouping. A single deterministic
64-bit root seed gives an input-support ceiling of $2^{64}$ for fixed settings;
domain-separated streams do not increase its entropy. Extra independent stochastic
keys count only if the population law declares them. Ideal continuous ranges do
not imply infinitely many distinguishable digital images. Finite precision,
conditional branches, clearance rejection, occlusion and pixel resolution reduce
realized variation.

The resolved population must include every active factor: architecture and
functional program; furniture, props and accepted support/interaction relations;
human bodies, clothing, hair and posed joints; assigned material/texture programs;
illumination and exposure; and captured camera intrinsics, extrinsics and times.
Compute joint descriptors on the same accepted rooms. Multiplying marginal
category counts or entropies assumes independence that the generator does not
have. Hidden material recipes, rejected objects, seed IDs and unsampled motion
endpoints contribute no observed diversity. Report conditional factor coverage
alongside the aggregate so broad lighting variation cannot conceal repeated
furnishings or people.

The current Mixed sampler (also accepted as \texttt{all}) proposes each of the
20 concrete room programs with probability $1/20$, including kitchens, dining,
teaching, transit, exhibition and public suites. A suite is one room draw even
when it contains several functional zones. Measure accepted proportions and
failure yield separately. Dataset defaults conditional on
\texttt{procedural-indoor} capture four $256\times256$ RGB views at progress zero.
Camera trajectories are enabled, but one captured timestep supplies no temporal
variation; ARDY and object motion are disabled unless requested. A longer path
does not multiply sample count. The three-view $512\times512$ library convenience
preset and the paper's recorded four-view, two-time gallery are different
populations and require distinct capacity receipts.

Geometry partitions should use primary-region topology and actual surface or
support descriptors, excluding random IDs and seeds. Appearance and learned
image descriptors need their own definitions. Pairwise distance below a threshold
is generally not an equivalence relation: connected-component chaining can join
visually different endpoints. Occupancy analysis therefore requires an explicit
partition, frozen independently of the final audit. Direct near-pair rates remain
separate diagnostics. Codebook identity, metric units, canonicalization and
out-of-domain behavior must accompany every estimate.

\subsection{Effective numbers and exact occupancy identities}
Following the effective-number interpretation of diversity~\cite{hill1973}, define
\begin{align}
 D_0 &= |\{j:p_j>0\}|, &
 D_1 &= \exp\!\left(-\sum_jp_j\log p_j\right), &
 D_2 &= \left(\sum_jp_j^2\right)^{-1}.
 \label{eq:capacity-hill}
\end{align}
These respectively describe support, entropy-equivalent balance and
collision-equivalent balance; $D_2\leq D_1\leq D_0$. They coincide for a uniform
finite population. A large rare tail can increase $D_0$ with little effect on
$D_2$. Multiplying marginal parameter counts or entropies ignores dependence.
For a correctly factorized program graph, joint entropy is
$H(Z)=\sum_k H(Z_k\mid\mathrm{parents}_k)$, and a deterministic rendering and
partition cannot increase entropy beyond the stochastic inputs.

For $n\geq1$ independent successful draws from the same population, let $U(n)$ be
expected occupied classes and $c_2=\sum_jp_j^2=1/D_2$. Indicator expectations give
\begin{align}
 U(n) &= \sum_j\left[1-(1-p_j)^n\right], \label{eq:capacity-occupancy}\\
 U(n+1)-U(n) &= \sum_j p_j(1-p_j)^n, \label{eq:capacity-novelty}\\
 \mathbb E[\text{equal-class pairs}] &= {n\choose2}c_2. \label{eq:capacity-pairs}
\end{align}
There is also a distribution-independent bound, for this fixed partition and IID law,
\begin{equation}
 \frac{n}{1+(n-1)c_2}\ \leq\ U(n)\ \leq\ \min(n,D_0).
 \label{eq:capacity-bound}
\end{equation}
To see the lower bound, let $N_j$ be sample class counts and $K_n$ their number of
nonzero entries. Cauchy--Schwarz gives $K_n\geq n^2/\sum_jN_j^2$. Jensen's
inequality and $\mathbb E\sum_jN_j^2=n+n(n-1)c_2$ give
Equation~\ref{eq:capacity-bound}. It is a bound, not an occupancy model inferred
from $D_2$ alone. The two known populations in Figure~\ref{fig:capacity-scaling}
have equal $D_2$ but different support and novelty curves.

With uniform support $S$, Equation~\ref{eq:capacity-occupancy} becomes
\begin{equation}
 U(n)=S\left[1-(1-1/S)^n\right]\simeq S\left(1-e^{-n/S}\right).
 \label{eq:capacity-uniform}
\end{equation}
The first repeated class and substantial saturation are different scales:
expected colliding pairs reach one near $\sqrt{2D_2}$, whereas uniform occupancy
saturates on scale $S$. Ten million draws with roughly 95\% distinct classes
would require about one hundred million equiprobable classes under this
particular model. Neither uniformity nor that support is established for
Zeroverse. The analytical table is generated in Rust from numerically stable
logarithm/exponential formulas, not fitted to captures.

Sampling without replacement requires a different law. For a finite seed pool
of size $M$ with class multiplicities $m_j$, replace $(1-p_j)^n$ by
$\binom{M-m_j}{n}/\binom{M}{n}$. Consecutive-seed regression audits are useful
deterministic coverage checks but do not by themselves justify IID confidence
claims. Stratified and family-grouped samples require their declared weights
and uncertainty method. Capture failures and retries must not silently change
the reported target population.

\subsection{Estimation and insufficient evidence}
Given IID class counts $n_j$ in $n\geq2$ observations,
\begin{equation}
 \widehat c_2=\frac{\sum_jn_j(n_j-1)}{n(n-1)}
 \label{eq:capacity-estimator}
\end{equation}
is unbiased for $c_2$; $1/\widehat c_2$ is not unbiased for $D_2$.
Pairs that share an observation are dependent. Treating all $\binom n2$ pair
indicators as independent binomial trials gives unjustified confidence bounds.
Zero observed matches must not produce a claim of infinite capacity.

A conservative fallback uses $m=\lfloor n/2\rfloor$ prespecified disjoint pairs
of independently sampled rooms. If none match, the exact one-sided
$1-\alpha$ upper confidence bound on $c_2$ and corresponding lower bound on $D_2$ are
\begin{equation}
 c_{2,\mathrm{upper}}=1-\alpha^{1/m},\qquad
 D_{2,\mathrm{lower}}=\frac{1}{1-\alpha^{1/m}}.
 \label{eq:capacity-zero}
\end{equation}
For 512 rooms and $\alpha=0.05$, this fallback yields only about 86 as a lower
bound on collision diversity, even when every observed class is distinct.
It is deliberately conservative, not an optimal use of all pairs. A stronger
dependency-aware estimator or a uniform birthday model needs its own justification.
No finite support upper bound follows from this observation without further
population assumptions, apart from any separately declared finite input ceiling.

Report observed support, singleton/doubleton counts, sample coverage, rarefaction
and held-out next-block novelty. Unseen-species theory permits useful bounded
extrapolation, but also establishes limits on distribution-free prediction
horizons~\cite{orlitsky2016}. It does not justify projecting hundreds of rooms
to ten million by fitting a convenient curve. Quantizers and thresholds must be
frozen before final evaluation; resampling and uncertainty operate on independent
room families. Ordinary bootstrap resampling of an all-unique observed set cannot
discover an unobserved rare tail. Adaptive stopping and multiple reported
configurations/resolutions also need an explicit confidence policy.

\subsection{Similarity, resolution scaling and learning}
For continuous image features, an accompanying sample diagnostic can use a
positive semidefinite similarity kernel with $K_{ii}=1$, such as
$K_{ij}=\exp(-\|x_i-x_j\|^2/(2\sigma^2))$ on frozen SigLIP2 features.
If $\lambda_i$ are eigenvalues of $K/n$, the Vendi score is
$\exp(-\sum_i\lambda_i\log\lambda_i)$; its order-two analogue is
$1/\sum_i\lambda_i^2$~\cite{friedman2023,pasarkar2024}. These measure similarity
diversity of the observed sample, depend on bandwidth and sampling, and are at
most $n$. They are not total generator support or the number of statistically
independent training examples. A linear feature kernel is additionally rank
limited by embedding dimension. Centered covariance rank and ellipsoid-volume
diagnostics have different meanings and must not be substituted for class counts.

At declared structural resolutions, a candidate local relation is
$\log D_2(\epsilon)\approx a-d\log\epsilon$. This is a resolution-scaling
hypothesis over a tested interval, not a global dimensionality theorem or a
sample-count law. Conditional branches and finite precision can produce plateaus
and changing slopes. Evaluate several frozen resolutions and held-out cohorts
before reporting an exponent. A separate downstream hypothesis,
\begin{equation}
 L(n)=L_\infty+A\,U(n)^{-\beta},
 \label{eq:capacity-learning}
\end{equation}
would require fixed model, optimizer, compute, real validation set and repeated
learning experiments. Compare it against raw-count and family-count alternatives;
SigLIP spread cannot identify $\beta$ or prove transfer. No fitted coefficients
for either scaling hypothesis are claimed here.

\subsection{Qualification and API contract}
The implemented capacity query binds resolved configuration, seed/failure law, sample
unit, exact engine/model identities, partition/kernel and resolution, confidence
policy, and requested sample horizon. It returns observed statistics, bounds or
explicitly model-conditional forecasts, with an optional point estimate and
nullable unresolved bounds. No matching evidence returns \emph{not qualified},
not a guessed dataset size. A read-only query must not initialize models or render
scenes; qualification is a separate explicit operation. These configuration-bound
API types live in the portable capture crate, the generator supplies the typed
configuration adapter, and the publication crate performs explicit offline
qualification and generates receipt-bound tables, figures and HTML. The initial
estimator supplies joint fixed-bin counts, observed-cohort rarefaction and exact
Clopper--Pearson collision bounds using disjoint room pairs. It uses a consistent
two-sided tail allocation, adjusted across prespecified definitions; choosing a
one-sided interval only after seeing zero matches would under-cover near that
selection boundary. Equation~\ref{eq:capacity-zero} remains the separately
prespecified one-sided analytical example. No unseen-tail forecast is promoted.
Native capture observation verifies full calibration, poses, frame delivery and
finite RGB/geometry/semantic buffers; CPU construction alone cannot qualify
rendered appearance. Bounded persistent workers preserve draw order and failed
attempts, and ordinary rendering pays no capacity-audit cost. The analytical
illustration command remains distinct from empirical qualification.

The optional image-space audit freezes the capture index, verified SigLIP2 weights,
preprocessing and RBF bandwidth before inference. It averages normalized view
embeddings within each room and normalizes the resulting centroid, giving every
room equal weight. The exact bounded eigensolver checks its residual and the
positive-semidefinite, trace-one spectrum of $K/n$; its Vendi and participation
scores describe the observed sample only. Complete fixed-seed replay is tested
separately from different-seed novelty. Whole-image embeddings include exterior
context and do not substitute for the primary-region geometry observer.

The validation sequence is: exact finite-population oracles; independent pilot
calibration of geometry/appearance equivalence; randomized structural audits;
nested rendered cohorts; held-out novelty forecasts; then fixed-budget downstream
learning. All views and times of a family stay together. Qualify whole-image and
primary-region measurements separately so background changes cannot stand in
for useful room diversity. Increase sample budgets only when interval precision
or unexplored strata require them, and expose the largest tested forecast horizon.
The Rust analytical fixtures enumerate small probability trees, verify occupancy
and collision identities, check rare-tail bounds and zero-match confidence
arithmetic, and regenerate the figure/table deterministically. They ground the
formulas; they do not experimentally qualify capacity of the rendered generator.

\section{Limitations}
Procedural correctness, broad parameter variation and efficient capture do not establish photographic realism. Hair is a surface groom, skin lacks a full subsurface model, some furnishings remain sparse, object motion uses approximate collision proxies, and diffuse GI uses probes and simplified transport. Glass and lighting are approximations rather than full path tracing. Transparent panes are excluded from opaque shadow and diffuse-transport geometry, so direct sunlight does not acquire physically complete tint, absorption or frosting redistribution. Geometric through-glass labels deliberately use straight rays rather than refracted RGB correspondence. Finite path-traced reference diagnostics still expose substantial scene-dependent transport error; the camera-baseline sweep does not measure physical accuracy or visual plausibility.

The current study is a generator audit and finite capture cohorts, not a ten-million-scene run. Process-lifetime memory stability, including retained graphics-driver allocations, is not qualified by these finite cohorts. The evaluation does not establish balanced coverage of all rare architectural combinations, collision-free arbitrary motion, suitability for every reconstruction loss, or transfer to real photographs. Shared-view defaults deliberately correlate cameras within a room; train/validation splits should therefore be made by room seed, not by image. A downstream assessment should freeze a training budget and real evaluation set, compare matched data quantities and camera policies, and measure reconstruction/calibration quality alongside redundancy. The manifests and metrics make that experiment reproducible without claiming its outcome in advance.
