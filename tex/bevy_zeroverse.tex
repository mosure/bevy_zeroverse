\begin{abstract}
We describe an open-source synthetic data engine combining primitive-object scenes with furnished procedural interiors, calibrated multi-view cameras, optional human motion, and aligned geometric annotations. Indoor generation samples continuous architectural, furnishing, appearance, lighting and camera parameters under geometric constraints. A generator-20 camera evaluation covers \CameraAuditRooms{} consecutive layouts and \CameraRenderRooms{} rendered rooms, including all-camera co-visibility, spatial spread and trajectory diversity. We retain the separate generator-18 evaluation of \AuditRooms{} layouts and \RenderRooms{} rendered rooms for appearance distributions, image-embedding variance and completed-capture throughput. Its measured CPU optimization reduces matched room-capture time by \SpeedupPercent{}\%, without reducing texture resolution, mesh detail or lighting samples. Published registry dependencies are measured separately from local graphics-library patches. These are generator and dataset diagnostics: photographic realism, unlimited-process stability and downstream pretraining gains are not established by this study.
\end{abstract}

\section{Purpose and scope}
Sparse-view reconstruction benefits from observations of shared surfaces: cameras that see unrelated objects offer little direct correspondence supervision. LRM-Zero demonstrates the potential of procedurally synthesized object data~\cite{xie2024}; MVSplat uses cross-view matching to estimate geometry~\cite{chen2024}. Our contribution is an inspectable data-generation system and its bounded evaluation, rather than a trained reconstruction model or a reconstruction accuracy result.

Version 0.24 retains the existing Object, CornellCube, Room, SemanticRoom and Human scene modes, and exposes furnished interiors separately as \texttt{ProceduralIndoor}. The runtime uses Bevy 0.19.1, optional Burn 0.21 inference, and published AnnyBody/ARDY integrations. Office, conference, lounge, classroom, library and related activity priors influence a compositional program; they do not select complete meshes or a finite collection of room templates. Most architecture, furniture, plants and clutter require neither mesh repositories nor photographic texture collections. AnnyBody human meshes and optional motion/embedding models are explicit pretrained dependencies.

\section{Procedural scene program}
A scene is generated as $S=G(s,\theta)$, where $s$ seeds independent random streams and $\theta$ contains user-controlled densities, activity, quality, camera and motion policies. The serialized manifest records the sampled program, object transforms and seeds, people, photometry and camera paths. A separate generator version and capture-engine identity invalidate incompatible resumptions. Determinism means reproducing the same program and settings with compatible assets and engine versions; bit-identical rasterization across GPU vendors is not promised.

\subsection{Architecture, objects and appearance}
Room area and aspect are sampled logarithmically; width and depth are clamped to 5.6--21 m and height spans 2.65--4.8 m. Furnishing zones have continuous bounds, orientation, aisle width, occupancy and mixed activity weights. Partitions sample portal width, thickness, glazing fraction, mullion spacing and optional transoms. Pillars, ceiling relief, paneling, reveals, trim and shelves add geometric structure. One to three exterior faces independently receive glazing; the fourth face retains the neighboring-room connection. Window opening dimensions, sill/head heights, bay widths, frames, inset depth and shades vary continuously. Rough openings are subtracted from the solid wall geometry; decorative surfaces respect these openings. The exterior envelope remains rectangular: these are varied rectilinear interiors, not a general architectural CAD system.

Furniture is assembled from material-specific parts. Tables vary outline, taper, thickness and leg/pedestal construction; chairs vary seat shell, curvature, back proportions, recline, arms, base and headrest. Sectional seating, shelving, potted plants, outlets, switches, ceiling lights, whiteboards, displays and tabletop objects extend the semantic inventory. Placement uses support relationships, clearance checks, zone-dependent furnishing fields and bounded rejection. Object yaw and arrangement are sampled rather than restricted to orthogonal grids. Collision proxies approximate detailed meshes; geometric validity is checked separately from physical plausibility.

Surfaces use 35 role-specific base material slots with procedural albedo, normal and roughness maps, metric UV scales and mip chains. Recipes vary spatial frequency, directional grain, textile structure, mineral veining, relief, wear, color and roughness. Object finish variants preserve their underlying maps while varying appearance; screens, boards and printed pages have independently seeded content. Only referenced finish variants are built during runtime preparation. Glass recipes vary roughness, refractive index, tint, attenuation and thickness. Direct lights vary placement, intensity, color temperature, circuit activity and direction; exposure, daylight and sky radiance are independently recorded. Low-light samples are retained in evaluation.

People retain AnnyBody geometry with continuous phenotype/pose parameters, fitted garments, surface hair and facial details. Static people remain the default. Opt-in ARDY motion combines seeded procedural text sequences with object-aware waypoint planning and scene-time playback. Requesting static scenes does not initialize motion inference. Motion models use the upstream loaders and caches; cached prompt text does not replace the motion seed. Clothing, hair, joint limits and collision handling are approximations, particularly during complex motion.

\subsection{Multi-view sampling and training defaults}
Camera centers and complete trajectories are constrained to the primary furnished room by default. The sampler varies focal length, position, target, roll and path shape, checks obstacle clearance and prefers visible content. Static capture uses one timestep; optional trajectories support repeated calibrated observations. A reference camera anchors a connected set of views. For each other camera and five trajectory times, we require
\[
 \min\{o_{0\rightarrow i}(t),o_{i\rightarrow0}(t)\}\geq\tau,
 \qquad b_{\min}\leq\|c_i(t)-c_0(t)\|\leq b_{\max},
\]
where $o$ estimates first-surface overlap using coarse visibility rays and object/architecture proxies. Overlap constrains reference edges, not every camera pair. Generator 20 additionally enforces $b_{\min}$ between every camera pair at 33 synchronized path samples. For three or more views, the horizontal camera-center covariance must have minor/major standard-deviation ratio at least 0.25 at those times. Paths independently vary heading, distance and curvature around the reference route; moving pairs require normalized RMS displacement difference of at least 0.15 after removing their starting offsets. Controls permit explicit rigid or collinear rigs. The finite proposal budget fails explicitly rather than silently weakening thresholds. Full-path swept collision checks remain; sampled spread and overlap are not continuous-time or pixel-level guarantees.

\section{Capture and annotation contract}
Capture begins only after scene preparation, assets, shader pipelines, lighting and requested human trajectories are ready. A bounded asynchronous readback protocol associates outputs with the requested scene, camera, time and mode; it does not infer readiness from an arbitrary number of frames. Geometric channels share an RGBA32Float ground-truth raster attachment. RGB uses Bevy's PBR pipeline, shadow maps, tone mapping and optional indirect-lighting approximation. Float32 storage does not imply that all RGB or lighting computations have float32 intermediate precision: HDR color and irradiance volumes include float16 stages.

Headless generation supports chunked safetensors or per-sample files, compression, resumable identity checks, metrics and bounded worker replacement. RGB and numeric channels have separate encoding contracts. Native generation performs numeric temporal export and O-Voxel export. The WebGPU viewer supports indoor construction, inspection and optional motion inference, using the same manifest and geometry modules; Portable quality reduces unsupported or costly rendering features. Browser velocity visualization is not a qualification of native dataset flow export. The current Wasm path omits native baked diffuse GI and uses its platform lighting fallback.

\section{Evaluation protocol and results}
The evaluation artifacts and commands are in \texttt{docs/generation\_v18.md}; machine-readable results generate this paper's tables. We audit \AuditRooms{} consecutive seeds starting at 24000 at density 0.65, human density 0.25 and four cameras, with no failed-seed replacement. The first \RenderRooms{} seeds are rendered at $384\times288$ with RGB, depth, normal, position and semantic outputs. Every scene contributes to the reports, including dark rooms and weak views. Geometric audits and rendered-camera checks answer different questions and are reported separately.

\subsection{Diversity and effective variance}
\input{generated/diversity_table.tex}
The full export includes \NumericSeries{} numeric distributions, \VaryingSeries{} of which have nonzero observed variance, \CategorySeries{} categorical distributions, zero-preserving per-class object counts, placement heatmaps, camera intrinsic/extrinsic distributions and joint histograms. These series are correlated projections of the program, not independent degrees of freedom. Histograms include their denominators and units; per-object style frequencies must not be interpreted as per-room frequencies.

For a categorical distribution $p$, we report the entropy-effective count $\exp(-\sum_jp_j\log p_j)$. For centered feature eigenvalues $\lambda_j$, we use $q_j=\lambda_j/\sum_k\lambda_k$ and entropy-effective rank $r_{\mathrm{eff}}=\exp(-\sum_jq_j\log q_j)$. Constant features have rank zero. We additionally measure normalized SigLIP2 base image embeddings~\cite{siglip2}, with frozen model/shard identity and declared resize/normalization. Nearest-neighbor distances exclude all views and times from the same scene. Scene-level variance uses one normalized mean embedding per room, so four cameras do not count as four independent scenes.

A seed's bit width does not establish dataset diversity. Continuous parameter ranges have no meaningful finite cardinality before quantization; topology, placement constraints, correlations and rendering further collapse distinguishable outcomes. The occupancy-signature estimate in Table~\ref{tab:diversity} ignores seeds and textures and uses a declared spatial quantization. It is a bounded geometric redundancy diagnostic, not proof of ten million useful examples. Embedding rank and nearest distances also depend on encoder, population size, viewpoint policy and illumination. The study does not identify a universal acceptable distance or train a downstream model.

\subsection{Generation performance}
\input{generated/performance_table.tex}
Timing uses completed samples, including asynchronous construction, lighting, rendering and readback. It excludes image/dataset encoding, validation and report writing unless explicitly labeled end-to-end. Each main benchmark runs 40 consecutive rooms, discards eight warmup rooms and captures four views per room at $320\times240$, one timestep, with static humans at density 0.25. Five-channel runs request RGB/depth/position/normal/semantic. Hardware is an NVIDIA RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell workstation GPU, Vulkan driver 610.43.02 on Linux. The benchmark records adapter, CPU, compiler, process memory, GPU timestamps and matching-PID NVML telemetry. Other desktop GPU activity is recorded, not attributed to the generator.

Profiling separates layout, camera planning, materials, geometry, tangent conversion and asset insertion. The old ``preparation'' field measured only the regeneration request, concealing the asynchronous work. The revised schema records both the request and actual CPU stages. Geometry is constructed once for transport and rendering; unused material variants are skipped; independent MikkTSpace mesh conversions use a bounded four-thread native pool. The development profile now optimizes the procedural crate at level 3 as well as dependencies. Wasm retains cooperative serial conversion. No mesh-detail, texture-resolution, GI-ray or view-count reduction is used in the comparison. The intermediate shared-geometry-only trial did not improve throughput and is retained in the evidence.

The before/after comparison changes both source preparation and the root development optimization level (1 to 3); it is not a source-only ablation or a release-LTO benchmark. Registry measurements compile the normalized crate archive without checkout-only wgpu patches. They should be used for published-package expectations. GPU timestamps are asynchronous diagnostic samples, and NVML active-process samples are not continuous occupancy measurements. Short runs cannot prove unlimited memory stability; finite child lifetimes remain part of the large-dataset operating policy. Throughput excludes neural motion generation and depends strongly on scene size, view count, resolution, modes and hardware.

\section{Subsequent gallery and viewer update}
The accompanying project page presents selected generator-20 renders separately from the population evaluations. Two example rooms (seeds 24005 and 24000) were captured at $768\times480$, with four synchronized cameras, three normalized trajectory times and all eight render modes. All mode switches reuse these exact scene/camera/time identities. The native CLI used Auto quality, static human density 0.25 and 1024 indirect-lighting rays per probe. Depth/position reconstruction and camera reprojection checks validate alignment before generating website previews. Display mappings for depth, normals, positions and temporal vectors are declared alongside the visualizations; they do not replace the lossless numeric exports.

Co-visibility shares the float32 surface raster used by the other geometric annotations. A GPU pass projects each source surface into the other cameras and checks frustum bounds and symmetric local tangent-plane agreement. The tolerance is $\max(1\text{ mm},10^{-4}d)$ at target depth $d$. The exact membership mask is separate from its additive color preview and source-valid mask; a valid but unshared surface differs from background even though both have a zero membership mask. This measures raster surface visibility, with the same annotation-opaque glass policy, rather than reflected or refracted RGB content.

Generator 19 also replaces the squared clothing chest offset with smooth body-derived cross-sections, localized folds and fitted garment details. Eight matched adult wardrobes were visually reviewed before and after the change. Backless stool variants were present in 89 of 128 audited rooms (201 of 1198 seats). Viewer pose joints can show through people while environment depth occludes them; editor projections survive regeneration. ARDY controls expose diffusion steps, history length, guidance and path conditioning, and the prompt program limits optional style modifiers. Local bone translations are interpolated continuously. The project page's separate motion example uses seed 13, two accepted trajectories and two cameras, rendered as 120 frames at the model's 20 Hz rate. It is a bounded demonstration, not a motion-quality benchmark. Native and WebGPU validation and remaining qualifications are recorded in \texttt{docs/viewer\_quality\_v19.md}.

\section{Limitations and remaining evidence}
Procedural correctness, broad parameter variation and efficient capture do not establish photographic realism. Hair is a surface groom, skin lacks a full subsurface model, some furnishings remain sparse, object motion uses approximate collision proxies, and diffuse GI uses probes and simplified transport. Glass and lighting are approximations rather than full path tracing. A retained earlier matched-scene Cycles study (generator 14, four unoccupied rooms, eight views, independent raw 8192-sample references) found coarse radiance relative errors of 9.0--98.7\%. That historical result is not a requalification of every generator-18 scene and explicitly includes the difficult dark example. Reference convergence and visual plausibility must be evaluated independently.

The current study is a generator audit and a small capture cohort, not a ten-million-scene run. It does not establish long-run resident-memory bounds, balanced coverage of all rare architectural combinations, collision-free arbitrary motion, suitability for every reconstruction loss, or transfer to real photographs. Shared-view defaults deliberately correlate cameras within a room; train/validation splits should therefore be made by room seed, not by image. A downstream assessment should freeze a training budget and real evaluation set, compare matched data quantities and camera policies, and measure reconstruction/calibration quality alongside redundancy. The manifests and metrics make that experiment reproducible without claiming its outcome in advance.
