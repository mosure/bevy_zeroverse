What does geometry coverage measure?

Six 24 × 24 orthographic depth maps describe each object's first surfaces, normalized by its fixed placement envelope. Distance averages depth differences over hit pixels; a missing surface costs one. Ordered greedy coverage counts representatives separated by a given distance. Three thresholds expose sensitivity. Color, texture and triangle counts do not increase coverage.

Counts are finite-sample diagnostics, not independent combination counts, SigLIP volume or a guarantee of learning utility. Thin details and flat content may be under-resolved. Saturated categories need a larger or finer audit. CPU build timings exclude scene placement, texture generation, uploads and rendering.

All samples pass finite mesh attributes, normal/winding, index, semantic and envelope checks. Those checks do not certify every contact, hidden self-intersection or physical construction. Room-level placement tests and the unfiltered room captures complement this isolated studio protocol.

Protocol, interpretation and limits

All 36 architectural/object roles are covered, with three floor types and a dedicated rug dye/pile program shown separately. Six fixed illustration seeds (0, 1, 4, 19, 53, 101) produce 234 native PBR views. The production material factory supplies every handle and texture. CPU reference maps, metric swatches, a fixed reflection environment, indoor exposure and fixed 1,400/250 lux studio lights isolate material appearance. This is not a throughput benchmark.

Twelve finish slots reuse one to three map structures per surface per room. Rug fields share at most four dedicated dye/pile maps. Room seeds resample the recipes. Shared maps preserve coherent finishes and bound preparation cost. Molded keyboard keys and printer shells use plastic, with charcoal, warm-white and colored pigments sharing dielectric microstructure. Painted metal is dielectric; chrome is conductive. Screens and prints use dedicated content programs. Optical constants for container glass and liquids include fixed values.

Map previews show level zero: base color precedes the material tint, roughness shows the packed green channel before its multiplier, and tangent normals differ from rendered view-space normals. A swatch cannot demonstrate photographic realism or perceptual embedding volume. The separate 256-room assignment cohort excludes humans and neighboring objects; walls, ceilings and facade privacy treatments are not part of that object denominator. Skin pigmentation and groom appearance require actor-specific qualification.

MAKE YOUR OWN CAPTURES

Start with shared views.

One seeded room, four cameras, RGB and matched geometry. Human motion is optional; this command does not initialize motion models.

Also available: primitive-object scenes, Cornell cube, room and semantic-room scenes, and standalone humans. The native CLI writes datasets; the WebGPU app is an interactive viewer.

cargo run -p bevy_zeroverse_burn --bin zeroverse_gen -- \
  --scene-type procedural-indoor --seed 7 \
  --samples 1 --workers 1 --cameras 4 \
  --width 768 --height 480 \
  --indoor-camera '{"baseline":0.5}' \
  --render-modes color depth normal semantic co-visibility \
  --ov-mode disabled --output-mode fs \
  --output out/shared_views --no-ui
